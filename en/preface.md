# Preface

Hello, I am Wang Honglei, from Chaoyang, Liaoning, China.

I have worked in operations and development for more than ten years. After entering the industry in 2011, I worked at Sina and Weibo on the reliability of large-scale applications and cloud-native platforms, platform development, and the construction of a group-wide private cloud. I later joined Shanghai Intelligent Computing Technology Co., Ltd., where I was responsible for building, delivering, operating, and developing a cluster with tens of thousands of accelerators for a national AI laboratory, supporting the long-term stability of large language model training and inference.

This book records what I learned and practiced during roughly my first year in AI infrastructure.

I am an engineer who gradually moved from traditional operations into this field, rather than an academic expert. Over the past year, I have worked across the AI infrastructure chain: AI data center construction, network planning, GPU selection, container clusters, training and inference platform development, training troubleshooting, and inference deployment. Along the way, I found that knowledge in this field is scattered, making it difficult for newcomers to develop an overall understanding. I wanted to organize that learning and practical experience into a reasonably complete map of AI infrastructure for those who follow.

To organize the knowledge an AI infrastructure engineer needs, I outlined 36 course categories with more than 600 lessons, covering mathematical foundations, programming languages, cloud-native systems, Linux operations, large model technology, reliability, and team management. This book distills those courses and practical experiences, together with some of my own judgments and interpretations.

It is therefore not a textbook and does not attempt to cover everything. It is closer to a practicing engineer's notebook: some topics receive detailed treatment, while others are outlined briefly. Some passages draw on substantial experience; elsewhere, I explicitly acknowledge that I am still exploring.

I am grateful to the friends, colleagues, readers of my WeChat public account, and family members whose support made this book possible.

I hope it provides useful guidance for recent graduates and junior to mid-level engineers in SRE, operations, and development. In particular, readers who want to enter AI infrastructure but do not know where to begin may find some direction here.

For questions or suggestions, you are welcome to follow my personal WeChat public account: wanghonglei.

My knowledge is limited. Please point out any errors or omissions you find.
